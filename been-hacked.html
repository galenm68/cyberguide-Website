Been Hacked?

Think Your Account or Device Has Been Compromised?

Don't panic. Acting quickly can help limit the damage and prevent further unauthorized access. Use the steps below to secure your information and begin recovering your account or device.

What to Do If an Account Is Compromised

1. Secure the Account

If you can still access the account:

  • Change your password immediately.
  • Sign out of other devices and active sessions.
  • Turn on MFA if it isn't already enabled.
  • Check your recovery email address and phone number.
  • Remove unfamiliar devices or login sessions.

Important: Change the password from a device you trust if you believe your computer or phone may also be compromised.

2. Check for Unauthorized Activity

Look through the account for anything you don't recognize.

Check for:

  • Unfamiliar login locations or devices
  • Messages you didn't send
  • Posts or purchases you didn't make
  • Changed account settings
  • New recovery information
  • Unfamiliar connected apps or services

3. Protect Your Other Accounts

If you reused the compromised password anywhere else, change those passwords too.

Start with accounts that could give an attacker access to other accounts, especially:

Email → Banking → School/Work → Social Media → Other Accounts

Your email account is especially important because it may be used to reset passwords for your other accounts.

What to Do If Your Device Is Compromised

Disconnect

If you believe malware is actively running on your device, disconnect it from the internet when practical. Turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the network connection.

Don't Make Things Worse

Avoid logging into sensitive accounts or entering passwords on a device you believe may be compromised.

Run a Security Check

Use your device's legitimate security tools to scan for malware. Remove suspicious applications or files when appropriate.

Update Your Device

Once you've addressed the immediate issue, install available security updates for your operating system and applications.

Get Professional Help

If you're unsure what happened or can't remove the problem, contact a trusted IT professional, your school's IT department, or the device manufacturer's support service.

Recovery Steps

1. Stop the Attack

Disconnect a potentially compromised device and stop interacting with suspicious messages, links, or websites.

↓
2. Secure Your Email

Change your email password and review recovery information and active sessions.

↓
3. Secure Other Accounts

Change passwords for accounts that used the same or similar credentials.

↓
4. Enable MFA

Add multi-factor authentication to important accounts.

↓
5. Check Your Information

Review financial transactions, account activity, messages, and other important records for unauthorized changes.

↓
6. Contact the Right People

Contact your bank, school, employer, service provider, or other organization if their systems or information may have been affected.

↓
7. Monitor Your Accounts

Continue watching for suspicious activity after you've regained control.

If Financial Information Was Stolen

If you notice unauthorized purchases or believe your financial information was exposed:

  • Contact your bank or credit card company using an official phone number.
  • Report transactions you don't recognize.
  • Consider replacing a compromised card.
  • Monitor your accounts for additional suspicious activity.
  • Be cautious of follow-up scams pretending to help you recover your money.

Never pay someone who unexpectedly contacts you and claims they can "recover" your account or money.

If You Can't Access Your Account

Use the Service's Official Recovery Process

Go directly to the company's official website or app and look for options such as:

  • "Forgot Password?"
  • "Can't access your account?"
  • "Account Recovery"

Avoid clicking recovery links sent through suspicious messages.

If the automated recovery process doesn't work, contact the service provider through its official support channels.

After You Recover

Getting your account back is only the first step. Take a few minutes to strengthen your security.

  • ☐ Change the compromised password
  • ☐ Change reused passwords on other accounts
  • ☐ Enable MFA
  • ☐ Remove unfamiliar devices and sessions
  • ☐ Check recovery email and phone information
  • ☐ Review recent account activity
  • ☐ Update your device and applications
  • ☐ Scan your device for malware
  • ☐ Review your security settings
  • ☐ Monitor the account for future suspicious activity

When to Get Help

You should consider getting professional assistance if:

  • You cannot regain access to an important account.
  • Your financial information was compromised.
  • Your device continues behaving strangely after a security scan.
  • You believe sensitive personal information was stolen.
  • Your school or workplace account may have been affected.
  • You suspect identity theft.

Remember: Act Fast, Stay Calm, Get Help.

A compromised account or device can be stressful, but you don't have to solve everything at once. Stop the threat, secure your accounts, recover your access, and monitor for additional activity.
